## PLATFORM PERSONALITY

Instagram is a visual-first platform where aesthetics do most of the first impression work, but copy closes the deal. The image or video earns the stop — the caption earns the relationship.

Users scroll fast. They make decisions in under two seconds. The hook in the first line of a caption is the only thing that gets read before "more" is tapped. Everything else is a bonus for engaged followers.

Instagram rewards:
- Consistent aesthetic identity
- Authentic personality in captions
- Value-first content (educational, entertaining, or emotionally resonant)
- Engagement in the first hour after posting (comments, saves, shares)

Instagram punishes:
- Inconsistency of any kind (visual, voice, posting frequency)
- Overt, unearned self-promotion
- Hollow captions that add nothing to the visual

## CAPTION ARCHITECTURE

### The Three-Zone Caption Model

**Zone 1: The Hook (Line 1)**
The only text visible before "more." Must earn the tap.
- Maximum 1 line (Instagram clips at approximately 125 characters before the truncation)
- Use the same hook taxonomy as `engines/writing-engine.md` — contradiction, surprising fact, bold opinion, specific scenario, specific question
- Never start with emojis as the first character (wastes the only character that matters)
- Never start with the account username tag or hashtag

**Zone 2: The Body (Lines 2–10 typically)**
Where the value, story, or message lives.
- 2–4 short paragraphs (1–3 sentences each)
- Use line breaks strategically — blank line between paragraphs
- Add emojis sparingly where they serve as visual breaks or emphasis, not decoration
- Deliver what the hook promised

**Zone 3: The Close / CTA (Last 1–2 lines)**
Where you invite engagement or direct action.
- Keep it specific and low-friction
- Match the register: a luxury brand does not say "Drop a comment below! 👇"
- Options: question, invitation, action, or a clean observation that needs no CTA

### Caption Length Guide
| Context | Length |
|---|---|
| Simple visual (food, landscape, product) | 1–3 lines |
| Lifestyle or aspiration | 3–8 lines |
| Personal brand / thought leadership | 8–20 lines |
| Educational (carousel companion) | 5–15 lines |
| Story post | 0–2 lines (support the visual; do not compete with it) |

## TONE BY ACCOUNT TYPE

### Personal Brand (Professional/Executive)
- Warm Professional or Intellectual/Thought Leader tone
- First-person, genuine
- More conversational than LinkedIn
- Shares perspective, not just achievement

### Business/Product Account
- Voice reflects the brand personality
- Always value-first (educate, entertain, inspire)
- Product mentions integrated naturally, not forced

### Luxury Brand
- Luxury/Premium tone profile (see `engines/tone-intelligence.md`)
- No exclamation points
- Understatement over superlative
- Let the visual carry the aspiration; copy provides texture

### Creator Account
- Warmth, personality, and specificity
- Audience feels like they know you
- Humor is permitted when authentic
- Never performs emotions — demonstrates them through specific content

## REEL SCRIPT FORMAT

### Hook (0–3 seconds)
This is the entire game. If the hook fails, no one sees the rest.

**Hook types for Reels:**
1. **Pattern interrupt** — Do something unexpected in frame
2. **Bold statement on screen text** — "I made $200K from one post" (then explain how)
3. **Direct address** — "If you're a [specific person], watch this"
4. **Problem identification** — "You're probably doing [X] wrong"
5. **Counter-narrative** — "Everyone says [common advice]. Here's why that's incomplete."

### Body (3 seconds – end)
- One point per scene or segment
- Keep pace moving — cuts every 2–4 seconds for retention
- On-screen text reinforces (does not repeat) the spoken word
- Maximum complexity appropriate to length:
  - 30-second Reels: one idea, one example
  - 60-second Reels: one framework, three components
  - 3-minute Reels: complete tutorial or story

### Close (Last 3–5 seconds)
- Call to action or memorable closing statement
- Do not end with silence — either keep talking or cut decisively

## CAROUSEL COPY

Carousels consistently outperform single images for reach and saves. The architecture:

**Slide 1:** Hook slide — the promise. What will the reader get by swiping through?
**Slides 2–8:** Delivery — each slide makes one point with one piece of supporting evidence or example
**Last slide:** Summary or CTA — what to do next

**Copy rules for carousels:**
- Headline text on each slide: 5–8 words maximum
- Supporting text (if any): 1–2 sentences
- Consistent visual rhythm — each slide should feel like it belongs
- Slide 2 is the most critical: it determines whether the reader swipes past slide 1

## BIO OPTIMIZATION

**Character limit:** 150 characters

**Bio architecture:**
```
[What you do — specific]
[Who you do it for — or: what they get from following you]
[One differentiator or credibility signal]
[Link-in-bio call to action]
```

**Example:**
```
Helping founders communicate like the leaders they are
Ex-McKinsey | 200K+ followers built in 18 months
↓ Free guide: Executive Communication in 5 Moves
```

**What never to put in an Instagram bio:**
- "Mom of 3 | Dog lover | Coffee addict" (irrelevant to professional brand)
- A laundry list of titles with no hook
- The link before the value proposition
- All lowercase or no punctuation when clarity requires both

## HASHTAG PHILOSOPHY

**Volume:** 3–5 focused hashtags (current best practice; avoid the old 30-hashtag approach)
**Placement:** End of caption or first comment (personal preference; both work)
**Selection:** Mix of:
- 1 broad hashtag (#Leadership, #Marketing) — discovery
- 1–2 niche hashtags (#ExecutiveCommunication, #StartupMarketing) — community
- 1 branded hashtag if applicable

**Do not:** Use irrelevant hashtags for reach (#love, #instagood on professional content)

---

## FORBIDDEN INSTAGRAM PATTERNS

1. Starting every caption with the same opener
2. Posting motivational quotes with no original commentary
3. Using too many emojis where text would be more impactful
4. Ending every post with "Save this for later!" — use it once, it works; use it every time, it becomes spam
5. Hiding hashtags in the caption mid-text (breaks readability)
6. DM-for-this-freebie tactics on professional/brand accounts (signals desperation)
7. Captions written in ALL CAPS for emphasis (reads as shouting)
8. Generic captions for a visual that deserved something specific
