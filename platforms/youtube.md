## PLATFORM PERSONALITY

YouTube is the internet's second-largest search engine and the primary platform for long-form video authority. Unlike TikTok (discovery-driven, short) or LinkedIn (network-driven, professional), YouTube is intent-driven: viewers arrive looking for something specific. This changes everything about how content must be positioned.

The YouTube viewer has chosen to spend time — typically 8–15 minutes, sometimes much more. They are making a real investment. The content must justify that investment with clear value, strong structure, and the credibility signals that come from genuine expertise.

YouTube rewards:
- Authority built through depth, not just credentials
- Consistency of niche and content style
- Search-optimized titles and descriptions
- High watch time and completion rate
- Subscriber trust built over multiple videos

## TITLE FORMULA

The YouTube title is an ad for the video. It must perform two jobs simultaneously: rank in search and earn the click from browsing.

### The Professional YouTube Title Formula

**Formula A: Curiosity + Clarity**
```
[What the viewer will know/be able to do] + [Specific context that earns interest]
```
Examples:
- "How I Closed Enterprise Deals Without a Sales Team (Year 2 Lessons)"
- "The Negotiation Framework That Changed Every Conversation I Have"
- "What Actually Happens to Your Brand When You Stop Posting for 90 Days"

**Formula B: Number + Specificity**
```
[Number] [Specific things] [That address a pain point or curiosity]
```
Examples:
- "5 LinkedIn Mistakes That Cost Me 3 Years of Growth (Avoid These)"
- "7 Email Habits of Executives Who Never Get Overwhelmed"
- "3 Decisions I Made That Turned Around a $2M Loss"

**Formula C: Counter-Narrative**
```
"[Common Belief] Is Wrong" / "The Truth About [Topic]" / "Why [Expected Thing] Doesn't Work"
```
Examples:
- "Why Having a Strong Personal Brand Is Actually Hurting Your Career"
- "The Truth About Thought Leadership Nobody Wants to Admit"
- "Why Your Email Marketing Isn't Working (It's Not What You Think)"

### Title rules
- 60 characters maximum (for full display in search)
- Include primary keyword naturally (not stuffed)
- Never use "Part 1" without "Part 2" already uploaded or committed
- No clickbait that the video does not deliver on — audience trust is destroyed in seconds

## DESCRIPTION ARCHITECTURE

The YouTube description is a conversion tool, an SEO asset, and a navigation guide for viewers who want to go deeper.

### Structure

**Paragraph 1: Hook (first 2–3 sentences)**
Visible before "Show more" is clicked. Must:
- State what the video is about in one sentence
- Give the viewer a reason to keep watching and a reason to read more
- Include the primary keyword naturally

**Timestamps (if video is 8+ minutes)**
```
00:00 Introduction
02:30 [Main Point 1]
07:15 [Main Point 2]
12:40 [Main Point 3]
18:00 Conclusion and next steps
```
Timestamps dramatically increase watch time by allowing viewers to navigate. They also contribute to YouTube's chapter markers feature.

**Body: Links and Resources**
- Any resources mentioned in the video
- Related videos (link to your own)
- Social media profiles
- Newsletter or lead magnet

**Keywords Paragraph (Bottom)**
A natural-language paragraph (not a keyword dump) that covers the video's main topics. Write for the reader first; SEO benefit comes from the keywords being present, not from optimizing for them.

## SCRIPT STRUCTURE

### Introduction Hook (First 30–60 seconds)
YouTube's most important structural element after the title. Viewers decide within the first 30 seconds whether to continue watching.

**Hook sequence:**
1. **State the problem or promise** — What will this video solve or deliver?
2. **Establish credibility** — Not "I am an expert in X" but one specific proof point: "I have managed 40 of these situations" or "I built three companies through exactly this challenge"
3. **Preview the structure** — "Today I'm covering A, B, and C. By the end, you'll have [specific outcome]."

**What not to do in the intro:**
- Long introduction about yourself before the value
- Asking people to like and subscribe before demonstrating value
- A cold open that has no connection to the title or thumbnail

### Body Structure
- Clear sections, each with a visible transition
- On-screen text reinforces key points
- B-roll or supplementary visuals break monotony in talking-head videos
- One primary idea per section
- Examples, stories, or data for each point — no abstract claims without illustration

### Conclusion
- Summary of core takeaways (not a repeat — a distillation)
- The "so what" — what to do with this information
- CTA: subscribe (with reason), watch next video, comment, or download resource
- End screen with recommended next video appearing in the final 20 seconds

## CHANNEL ABOUT SECTION

**Purpose:** Convert channel visitors into subscribers. This is your permanent first impression for anyone researching you.

**Structure:**
```
[Who you are — one specific sentence with a differentiator]
[What content you create and for whom]
[Why someone should subscribe — specific value they get]
[Upload schedule if consistent]
[Links: primary social, newsletter, website]
```

**Example:**
```
I've led product teams at three companies through IPO, acquisition, and failure.
On this channel: the real mechanics of product leadership — not the sanitized version.
New video every Thursday. No filler content.
→ LinkedIn: [link]
→ Newsletter: [link]
```

## COMMUNITY POSTS

Short-form text + image posts visible to subscribers. Used for:
- Engagement polls between videos
- Early announcements before a new video
- Short insights that do not warrant a full video
- Behind-the-scenes content

**Community post format:**
- 100–300 words maximum for text posts
- Question format for polls
- Images that are self-explanatory (not just a thumbnail)

---

## THUMBNAIL TEXT PRINCIPLES

Thumbnail text is not caption territory — it is impact territory.

**Rules:**
- Maximum 4–6 words of text on the thumbnail
- Text must be immediately readable at thumbnail size (100×75px equivalent)
- No small print
- Contrast must be absolute — white text on dark, or dark text on light with a visible outline
- The text should complement the title, not repeat it

**What the thumbnail text should do:**
- Tease the most surprising or valuable element of the video
- Create an emotional reaction (curiosity, recognition, urgency)
- Pair with a facial expression or visual element that amplifies it

## PLAYLIST TITLES AND DESCRIPTIONS

Playlists are a navigation and retention tool — they keep viewers watching multiple videos in a series.

**Playlist title formula:** [Topic] — [What Viewer Gets]
Example: "Executive Communication — Master Every High-Stakes Conversation"

**Playlist description:** 2–3 sentences explaining what the playlist covers and who it is for. Include primary keywords.

---

## FORBIDDEN YOUTUBE PATTERNS

1. Asking for likes/subscribes before delivering any value
2. Long, meandering intros ("So today, guys, I wanted to talk about something that I've been thinking a lot about lately...")
3. Titles that do not match video content (destroys trust permanently)
4. Clickbait thumbnails that misrepresent the video
5. Videos longer than the content justifies
6. Inconsistent upload schedule without acknowledgment
7. Descriptions with only "Follow me on Instagram!" — missed SEO and navigation opportunity
8. No timestamps on videos longer than 8 minutes
