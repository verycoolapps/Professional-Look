## PLATFORM PERSONALITY

TikTok is the attention economy at its most distilled. The algorithm is brutally objective: it shows content to a small initial audience, measures engagement rate, and distributes accordingly. No followers required to reach millions. No legacy reputation to coast on.

For professional communicators, TikTok is a discovery platform — one of the few places where an unknown professional can build an audience without an existing network. The trade-off: the platform rewards authenticity, speed, and entertainment far more than polish or credentials.

The first three seconds of a TikTok video determine whether it succeeds or fails. Everything else is optimization.

## THE HOOK — 0 TO 3 SECONDS

This is the single most important element in any TikTok. If the hook fails, nothing else matters.

### What a hook must do:
1. **Stop the scroll** — Create enough pattern interruption or interest that the viewer does not swipe up
2. **Promise something** — Explicitly or implicitly communicate that watching the rest will be worth their time
3. **Be immediate** — Start at the beginning of the value, not at the beginning of the context

### Hook types for TikTok

**Type 1: The Provocative Opening Statement**
Start with a claim that is bold enough to be interesting but credible enough to believe.
Example: "Most career advice I've heard is actively harmful."

**Type 2: The Direct Problem Identification**
Call out a specific situation your audience is in.
Example: "If you're struggling to get responses after job interviews, this is why."

**Type 3: The Visual Hook**
Something unexpected in the frame in the first second — holds attention until the viewer understands what is happening.

**Type 4: The Open Loop**
State the payoff at the start but delay the mechanism.
Example: "I grew from 0 to 80,000 followers in 4 months. Here's the only thing that actually mattered."

**Type 5: The Counter-Narrative**
Start by challenging something the audience believes.
Example: "Everyone says to 'be yourself' in interviews. That advice will cost you the job."

### On-screen text hooks
The text on the screen in the first 1–2 seconds often does more work than the spoken word. It should either mirror and reinforce what is said or add a second layer of information. It should never compete with or contradict the spoken word.

## SCRIPT STRUCTURE

### 30-Second Format
**Purpose:** One idea, one insight, clear delivery.
```
0–3s: Hook (visual + spoken)
3–10s: Set up the context briefly (what this is about)
10–20s: The core content (the one thing)
20–27s: The evidence or example
27–30s: The CTA or close
```

### 60-Second Format
**Purpose:** One framework with multiple components, or a story with a reveal.
```
0–3s: Hook
3–10s: Promise (what the viewer gets from watching all of this)
10–40s: The content — 2–3 points maximum, each with one example
40–55s: The conclusion or summary
55–60s: CTA
```

### 3-Minute Format (Education or Story)
**Purpose:** Complete tutorial, detailed framework, or a story that requires context.
```
0–3s: Hook
3–15s: Context and promise
15–150s: The content — structured in clear, labeled segments
150–165s: Summary or main takeaway
165–180s: CTA and optional invitation to comment
```

### Pacing principles
- Change in frame or camera angle every 2–4 seconds (for retention)
- On-screen text changes reinforce the pacing
- Never let a silent or static moment exceed 2 seconds unless intentional
- The end card should appear before the viewer can swipe

## EDUCATIONAL STORYTELLING

Education is the highest-performing content category for professional TikTok because the platform's algorithm favors completion rate, and educational content earns it.

### The educational TikTok structure:
1. **The problem statement** — A situation the audience is in or wants to understand
2. **The insight** — The thing that resolves or illuminates the problem
3. **The evidence** — A specific example, statistic, or story
4. **The application** — What the viewer can do with this information
5. **The close** — Either a call to action or a memorable line

### What makes educational TikTok work:
- Specificity over generality
- Practical application over theory
- The viewer's situation, not the creator's expertise
- One idea done completely, not five ideas done partially

## CREATOR VOICE

TikTok audience can detect scripted content that has been over-produced into sterility. The voice should feel:
- **Spoken, not written** — the words should sound like something a person would actually say
- **Direct** — talking to one person, not performing for a crowd
- **Specific** — using real numbers, real examples, real situations
- **Energized without being manic** — genuine engagement, not artificial enthusiasm

### Voice calibration for professional TikTok
Professional accounts should calibrate between Warm Professional and Intellectual/Thought Leader tone profiles — not Corporate or CEO/Founder (too distant for TikTok's intimacy expectation).

The best professional TikTok voice: "Smart person talking clearly about something they actually know, directly to you."

## ON-SCREEN TEXT OPTIMIZATION

On-screen text is a critical element of TikTok's professional content.

**Rules:**
- Maximum 6–8 words per text card
- High contrast between text and background (white text on dark overlay or vice versa)
- Change text when the spoken content changes — text should track the narrative
- Do not display text while the viewer is still processing the previous text
- Summary statements in text work especially well for retention (viewer reads + hears = reinforced)

## CTA PLACEMENT

**Best positions for CTA in TikTok:**
1. Verbal CTA in the last 5 seconds
2. On-screen text CTA in final frame
3. Caption CTA (under the video) — for follow/comment invitation
4. Comment pinned response — for FAQ, resources, or follow-up

**Professional CTAs that work:**
- "Follow for [specific content type]" — only if you can be specific about what follows
- "Comment [word] and I'll send you [resource]" — if you can deliver
- "Part 2 if you want [specific next step]" — commitment to follow through

**Professional CTAs that fail:**
- "Like and subscribe" — sounds like 2015 YouTube
- "Share this with someone who needs to hear it" — hollow if the content was not remarkable
- No CTA at all — loses the follow opportunity after a good video

## CAPTION BREVITY

TikTok captions are largely invisible to most viewers but are read by:
- People who found the video through search
- Viewers who want more context after watching

**Rules:**
- Maximum 150 characters for professional accounts
- Include 2–3 relevant keywords (for search discovery)
- Optional CTA if the video did not include one
- No hashtag stuffing — 3–5 relevant hashtags at most

## BIO OPTIMIZATION

**TikTok bio: 80 characters**

```
[What you create / what you do]
[Who it is for]
[Optional: proof or differentiator]
[Link or "link in bio" reference]
```

Example:
```
Making complex business strategy actually useful
For founders + executives building fast
↓ Free strategy guide
```

---

## FORBIDDEN TIKTOK PATTERNS

1. Voiceover-only with no visual movement — kills retention
2. Long pre-hook context ("So today I wanted to talk about...") — starts with the wrong information
3. Reading from a teleprompter without natural delivery — visible scripting breaks trust
4. Overproduced content with no personality — polish is not the goal
5. Posting low-effort content and expecting the algorithm to perform it
6. Posting only trend-following content without original perspective
7. Ending with silence and no close
8. Posting at inconsistent intervals (algorithm penalizes inactivity)
