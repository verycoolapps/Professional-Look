## PLATFORM PERSONALITY

LinkedIn is the only major social platform where professional credibility is the currency. Users are there to build careers, find opportunities, learn, and signal expertise. The audience is self-selected for professional interest — they are not scrolling to be entertained; they are scrolling to gain advantage.

This creates specific rules for what works:
- Content that provides concrete professional value earns engagement
- Content that is obviously self-promotional without value earns resentment
- Content that builds genuine authority — by demonstrating expertise through specific insight — earns the highest form of LinkedIn equity: trust

The median LinkedIn user reads fast and decides within three lines whether to expand a post. The hook is not optional — it is the entire game.

---

## AUDIENCE EXPECTATIONS

**What LinkedIn audiences expect:**
- Content that respects their intelligence and professional experience
- Specific insight, not generic advice
- Honesty about how things work, not how they are supposed to work
- Professional achievement presented without explicit boasting
- Commentary on industry dynamics that reflects actual knowledge

**What will hurt performance:**
- Motivational content with no professional specificity
- Humble-brags that go wrong ("I turned down a $5M deal because principles")
- Engagement bait that insults reader intelligence
- Political content unrelated to professional domain
- Content clearly produced without genuine thought

## POST FORMATS

### Short Post (150–300 words)
The most effective format for building authority and driving engagement.

**Structure:**
```
[Hook — 1–2 lines, must earn the expand click]

[Body — 2–4 paragraphs, each with one clear point]

[Close — 1–2 lines: observation, question, or invitation]
```

**LinkedIn-specific formatting:**
- Use line breaks between paragraphs (blank line between each)
- No markdown headers (not rendered on LinkedIn)
- Bullet points and numbered lists work well mid-body
- Maximum 5 hashtags; minimum 2 if hashtags are used at all; place at the end

### Long-Form Post (400–700 words)
For deeper insight, storytelling, or comprehensive frameworks.

**When to use:**
- The story requires context that cannot be compressed
- The framework has enough components to warrant length
- The author's authority on this specific topic is high enough to earn the read

**Risk:** Most long-form posts fail because writers confuse length with depth. Every word must add value. If a long-form post at 400 words is as complete as it would be at 700, the 400-word version is better.

### LinkedIn Article
Evergreen, SEO-indexed, professional depth. 800–2000 words.

**Best for:**
- Comprehensive frameworks or methodologies
- Industry analysis with data
- Thought leadership that warrants permanent, linkable reference
- Content that will be shared as a resource, not just as a feed post

### LinkedIn Poll
Simple, high-engagement, low-effort for audience.

**Best for:** Market research, audience segmentation, generating conversation around a question you genuinely want to answer.

**Rules:** The question must be genuinely interesting. Do not create polls designed to make you look good regardless of outcome.

### Carousel Post
High engagement, swipeable, visual.

**Best for:** Frameworks, numbered lists, before/after comparisons, step-by-step guides.

**Rules:** Slide 1 is the hook. Slide 2 must justify slide 3. Every slide must earn the swipe. Maximum 10 slides.

## EXECUTIVE BRANDING BEST PRACTICES

### The Authority Equation
Authority on LinkedIn = Specific expertise + Consistent presence + Genuine perspective

**What builds authority:**
1. Posts that demonstrate you know things others in your field do not
2. Predictions and positions that you can defend with evidence
3. Honest reflection on failure and what it produced
4. Specific data, findings, or observations from your actual work
5. Commentary on industry news that offers analysis, not just acknowledgment

**What erodes authority:**
1. Generic motivational content
2. Sharing others' content without meaningful commentary
3. Engagement bait disguised as professional content
4. Credential lists without demonstrated insight
5. Over-posting without quality control

### Thought Leadership Formula
Thought leadership is not a posting strategy — it is what happens when a specific person with specific knowledge shares a specific perspective. The formula:

```
SPECIFIC KNOWLEDGE + UNIQUE ANGLE + CLEAR POSITION + SPECIFIC EVIDENCE
= Genuine thought leadership
```

Every "thought leadership post" that lacks one of these four ingredients is content marketing, not leadership.

## ENGAGEMENT TRIGGERS

**What drives comments on LinkedIn:**
1. A post that makes a claim people want to agree or disagree with
2. A question at the end that is genuinely answerable from professional experience
3. A story that reveals something most readers have experienced but not articulated
4. A framework or list that sparks reactions ("I'd add X" or "You missed Y")
5. An honest admission that challenges the norm of polished professionalism

**What drives shares:**
1. Content that makes the sharer look good for sharing it
2. Data, statistics, or research that others can use
3. Frameworks that are immediately applicable
4. Honest takes that others felt but did not say

## HASHTAG STRATEGY

- Use 2–5 hashtags per post
- Place hashtags at the very end of the post, after the last line
- Do not use hashtags in the middle of sentences
- Mix: one broad hashtag (#Leadership), one industry hashtag (#TechStrategy), one specific hashtag (#ProductManagement)
- Avoid single-use invented hashtags with low search volume

## PROFILE OPTIMIZATION

### Headline
- Not just your title — your value proposition
- Format: [Title] | [What you do for whom] | [One specific differentiator]
- Example: "VP Product | Building AI-native products that reduce enterprise churn | 3× product launches, 0 to $50M ARR"
- Not: "VP of Product at [Company Name]"

### About Section (Summary)
- First two lines visible before "See more" — make them earn the click
- Maximum 2,000 characters; optimal length: 1,000–1,500
- Third person or first person consistently; never switch
- End with a specific invitation (what to reach out about)

### Featured Section
- Lead with your most valuable piece of content or most credibility-building achievement
- Maximum three items; prioritize quality over completeness

## SPECIFIC POST TYPES

### Achievement Announcement (The Quiet Flex)
**DO NOT:** "Excited to share that I just closed our biggest deal of the year! So grateful to the team."
**DO:** Start with the insight that got you there. The achievement becomes evidence, not the point.

### Job Transition Announcement
- Lead with what you are moving toward, not what you are leaving
- Name what you learned in the previous role specifically
- Express gratitude without sycophancy
- Keep it shorter than you think it needs to be

### Crisis or Difficult Situation
- Acknowledge first; explain second; defend last (or not at all)
- Never post defensively in the heat of the moment
- See `engines/tone-intelligence.md` Crisis/Sensitive profile

### Comment Writing
- Comments that add perspective are rewarded with visibility
- Do not say "Great post!" — it adds nothing and signals you read the headline
- Disagree respectfully when you genuinely disagree; this builds more credibility than agreement
- Ask a question that follows logically from the post; shows you read it

### Connection Request Messages
- Personalize every request: reference a specific post, mutual connection, or genuine reason
- Maximum 2–3 sentences: who you are, why you are connecting, what you offer or are curious about
- Do not pitch on first contact; never pitch in a connection request

## FORBIDDEN LINKEDIN PATTERNS

1. "I'm excited/thrilled/honored to announce..." — Remove; start with the announcement
2. "#Blessed" — Never on LinkedIn
3. Motivational quotes without commentary — Low value
4. "Thoughts?" as the only engagement invitation — Add a specific question
5. Posting the same article link five times in a month without new commentary each time
6. Writing in third person about yourself in posts ("CEO John Smith recently...")
7. Long lists of emojis in business content
8. Mass tagging people who are not mentioned in the post
9. "I need 10,000 followers to post this" or any follower threshold content
10. Posting salary surveys or asking others what they earn on public posts
