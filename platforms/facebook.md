## PLATFORM PERSONALITY

Facebook's professional communication landscape is different from LinkedIn's in one fundamental way: Facebook has a wider demographic range. Its professional users skew older than Instagram or TikTok, their networks are a blend of professional and personal connections, and the content that performs tends to have an emotional or community dimension that purely professional content lacks.

Facebook is not where thought leadership is primarily built — it is where community is maintained and conversations happen. For businesses, it is a local and relational platform. For organizations, it is an announcement channel to an established audience. For professional individuals, it is where career achievements land alongside life updates.

The tone is warmer and more personal than LinkedIn. The audience expects to feel something, not just learn something.

## AUDIENCE EXPECTATIONS

**Facebook audience characteristics:**
- Mixed professional and personal connections — the post lives in both worlds
- Broader age range: strongest professional demographic is 30–55
- Higher tolerance for personal context in professional posts
- More emotional register expected
- Community and relationship content over pure expertise signals
- Events, local businesses, and organizations perform particularly well

**What works on Facebook:**
- Stories with emotional resonance
- Community updates and milestones
- Organizational announcements with human elements
- Event promotion with clear, specific details
- Business updates framed around what they mean for the community
- Content that invites personal experience-sharing

**What underperforms:**
- Pure thought leadership without personal element (better on LinkedIn)
- Purely promotional content without community value
- Technical content without accessible framing
- Very long text without visual support

## COMMUNICATION CONTEXTS

### Personal Professional Posts (Individual)
A professional sharing career news, milestones, or insights with their personal network.

**Tone:** Warm Professional — more personal than LinkedIn
**Structure:** Personal context → Professional fact → Reflection or gratitude
**Length:** 50–200 words
**Example use cases:** Job changes, promotions, project completions, professional reflections, sharing of work relevant to personal connections

### Organizational Announcements (Business/Nonprofit)
Official communications from a business or organization to its Facebook page followers.

**Tone:** Warm Corporate or Leadership — human but representing the organization
**Structure:** What → Why it matters to the community → What's next
**Length:** 100–300 words
**Example use cases:** New product or service announcements, policy changes, community initiatives, event announcements

### Community Posts (Group or Page Engagement)
Content designed to generate discussion, answer questions, or engage a specific community.

**Tone:** Warm Professional — conversational and accessible
**Structure:** Topic or question → Brief context → Open invitation for contribution
**Length:** 30–100 words (shorter invites more participation)
**Example use cases:** Discussion prompts, FAQ responses, community polls, shared resources

### Event Promotion
Specific announcement with actionable details.

**Structure:**
- What (specific name of event)
- When (specific date, time, timezone)
- Where (location or link)
- Why this matters to the audience
- How to participate or register
- One visual element (image or graphic)

**Length:** 50–150 words (the details carry the weight, not the prose)

## POST STRUCTURES

### Emotional Hook + Professional Fact
Works for personal milestones and career announcements.
```
[One sentence that connects emotionally to the experience]
[The professional fact]
[Reflection on what it means]
[Optional: gratitude or invitation]
```

### Community Update Frame
Works for organizational and community posts.
```
[What happened / is happening]
[What it means for people in this community]
[What to do or expect next]
[How to get involved if relevant]
```

### Value-First Post
Works for expertise content adapted to Facebook's emotional register.
```
[Relatable opening — a situation most readers have experienced]
[The useful insight]
[A specific, actionable next step]
[Closing that invites personal experience-sharing]
```

## BUSINESS PAGE BEST PRACTICES

### Posting frequency
- 1–2 times per week for active presence
- 4–5 times per week for growth-focused business pages
- Consistency matters more than frequency

### Content mix (80/20 rule)
- 80%: Content that provides value to the audience (educational, entertaining, relatable)
- 20%: Promotional content

### Engagement tactics
- Respond to every comment within the first 4 hours — Facebook's algorithm rewards engagement
- Ask questions at the end of posts to invite responses
- Use Facebook's native features (polls, events, live video) — native content gets preferential reach

### Local business specifics
- Tag location in every post
- Reference the local community by name
- Share community involvement and local partnerships
- Highlight local testimonials and reviews

## FACEBOOK ADS COPY (Brief)

Facebook ad copy follows a distinct structure from organic posts.

**Primary text (above the visual):**
- Lead with the pain point or desire, not the product
- Maximum 2–3 sentences before "see more" cutoff
- Be specific about what the audience gets

**Headline (below the visual):**
- 5–7 words
- Value proposition or clear offer

**Description (below headline):**
- Supporting context or urgency trigger

**CTA button:** Use specific options ("Learn More," "Book Now," "Shop Now") over generic ("Get Started")

## FORBIDDEN FACEBOOK PATTERNS

1. Sharing links without any contextual commentary (pure link posts perform poorly algorithmically)
2. Posting identical content from other platforms without adapting (LinkedIn posts often look robotic on Facebook)
3. All-caps text anywhere in the post
4. Generic "Happy Monday!" or day-of-week posts without substantive content
5. Asking for likes/shares explicitly ("Please share this post!")
6. Tagging non-relevant people to boost visibility (can trigger spam flags)
7. Posting confidential internal information
8. Responding to negative reviews publicly in a defensive tone
9. Using Facebook to air workplace grievances
