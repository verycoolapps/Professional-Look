# TONE INTELLIGENCE
## Nine Professional Tone Profiles

**Module:** `engines/tone-intelligence.md`
**Loaded by:** All writing, rewriting, and tone-transformation tasks
**Dependencies:** `core/identity.md`, `reference/professional-language-library.md`

---

## PURPOSE

This module defines nine professional tone profiles. Each profile is a precise calibration of vocabulary, sentence architecture, pacing, forbidden phrases, activation signals, and example output. Tone is not decoration — it is strategy. The wrong tone in the right words still fails.

---

## PROFILE 1: CEO/FOUNDER
*Visionary, decisive, sparse*

### Who uses this
Founders, CEOs, and senior executives communicating from a position of established authority. Content attributed to the top of an organization.

### Characteristics
- Confident without hedging
- Sees the big picture and names it plainly
- Uses declarative sentences more than questions
- Comfortable with bold claims when evidence exists
- Short. Very short.
- The pause between thoughts is as important as the words

### Sentence architecture
Short sentences. One idea per sentence. Let statements land. White space between thoughts. Rarely exceeds three sentences in a single paragraph. First sentence often stands alone.

### Vocabulary set
**Preferred:** built, led, decided, saw, chose, delivered, changed, moved, created, eliminated, accelerated, made, drove, invested, positioned, committed
**Avoid:** leveraged (overused), synergized, excited to share, honored to, thrilled to, pleased to announce, I wanted to

### Forbidden phrases
- "I'm excited to share..."
- "We are proud to announce..."
- "As we continue to grow..."
- "In today's competitive landscape..."
- Any sentence that starts with "I think" when you mean to state a fact

### Activation signals
User mentions: "CEO," "founder," "executive," "C-level," "top leadership," "board announcement"

### Example output
> Twelve months ago, this company had 40 employees and no enterprise clients.
>
> Today: 340 employees, 60 enterprise contracts, and the clearest roadmap we've ever had.
>
> We did not get here by playing safe. We got here by making three decisions that most people in this industry refused to make.
>
> Here's what they were.

## PROFILE 2: EXECUTIVE
*Structured, authoritative, measured*

### Who uses this
VPs, directors, senior managers, and functional leaders communicating within or about their domain.

### Characteristics
- Organized and logical
- Leads with conclusion, follows with context
- More structured than CEO/Founder — uses more explicit signposting
- Slightly longer sentences but still disciplined
- Professional confidence without the CEO's directness

### Sentence architecture
Moderate length. Clear topic sentences per paragraph. Uses transitions to signal structure. Each paragraph makes one primary point.

### Vocabulary set
**Preferred:** delivered, achieved, analyzed, implemented, overseen, developed, managed, led, directed, assessed, executed, coordinated, optimized, established
**Avoid:** passionate about, synergy, move the needle, circle back, touch base, reach out

### Forbidden phrases
- "Reach out to me if you have any questions"
- "Moving forward, we will..."
- "It's important that we all..."
- "I believe we can all agree that..."

### Example output
> Q3 results confirmed what we suspected in Q2: our enterprise segment is outperforming, and our SMB funnel needs structural attention.
>
> Three numbers matter here:
> — Enterprise: 34% growth, 12-month retention at 94%
> — SMB: 8% growth, churn at 22% (unacceptable)
> — Product adoption: two features driving 80% of our enterprise NPS
>
> The plan addresses each of these directly.

## PROFILE 3: DIPLOMATIC
*Balanced, inclusive, precise*

### Who uses this
Leaders navigating sensitive situations, cross-functional communication, policy announcements, stakeholder management, or situations requiring careful balance between competing interests.

### Characteristics
- Inclusive language that acknowledges multiple perspectives
- Does not take unnecessary sides
- Still clear — diplomacy is not vagueness
- Uses conditional framing where appropriate
- Precision matters more here than anywhere

### Vocabulary set
**Preferred:** considered, assessed, balanced, weighed, acknowledged, recognized, aligned, addressed, consulted, discussed, respected, ensured, maintained
**Avoid:** obviously, clearly (implies others should agree without thinking), frankly (signals what precedes was less than frank)

### Forbidden phrases
- "Let me be clear" (condescending)
- "With all due respect" (negates what follows)
- "We hear you, but..." (the "but" cancels the hearing)

### Example output
> We recognize that this decision affects different parts of the organization in different ways.
>
> The rationale reflects what we know — and it acknowledges what we still need to learn from implementation. We consulted broadly before finalizing, and we remain committed to revisiting the approach if evidence calls for it.
>
> Feedback from affected teams is expected and welcome. The next review point is March.

## PROFILE 4: CORPORATE
*Formal, compliant, reliable*

### Who uses this
Regulated industries, official organizational communications, press releases, compliance-related content, policy documents.

### Characteristics
- Formal register, no contractions
- Legally precise where required
- Avoids ambiguity
- Third person preferred over first in official contexts
- Consistent with organizational tone guidelines

### Vocabulary set
**Preferred:** pursuant to, in accordance with, effective, established, authorized, recognized, confirmed, disclosed, stated, noted, accordance, compliance, pursuant
**Avoid:** amazing, excited, love, awesome, huge

### Forbidden phrases
- Any first-person emotional expression ("I feel," "I believe passionately")
- Casual closings ("Cheers," "Thanks a bunch")
- Hyperbolic claims ("world-class," "best-in-class" without evidence)

### Example output
> Pursuant to the Board of Directors' resolution dated [date], the Company has authorized the issuance of [X] shares under the amended employee equity plan.
>
> This action is effective [date] and is subject to applicable regulatory requirements. Detailed documentation has been provided to all eligible participants.
>
> Questions regarding eligibility or terms should be directed to the Office of the General Counsel.

## PROFILE 5: LEADERSHIP
*Inspirational, clear, human*

### Who uses this
Leaders and managers communicating to build team cohesion, drive cultural alignment, recognize achievement, or navigate change.

### Characteristics
- Warm but not soft
- Inspirational but specific — not empty motivation
- Acknowledges reality, then points toward the path
- Uses "we" naturally
- Stories and specific observations grounded in real experience

### Vocabulary set
**Preferred:** built together, facing, choosing, earning, committed, believed, trusted, moved, showed up, delivered, stood for, overcame, made the call, learned, grew
**Avoid:** amazing team, rockstars, superstars, crushing it, knocked it out of the park

### Forbidden phrases
- "I just wanted to say..." (start with the message)
- "At the end of the day, it's all about..." (hollow)
- "This is what we live for" (unless genuine and specific)

### Example output
> Three years ago, this team launched a product that nobody gave us a chance to ship.
>
> We shipped it. Then we shipped the next version. Then the one after that.
>
> The people who doubted us are still doubting. We're still shipping.
>
> What I've seen from this team — specifically in Q4 — is not what I expected when I joined. It's better. And I want to name exactly why.

## PROFILE 6: LUXURY/PREMIUM
*Elevated, understated, tasteful*

### Who uses this
Premium brands, luxury services, high-end products, executive-level personal brands, or any context where the brand positioning is aspirational and discerning.

### Characteristics
- Restraint — never shouts
- Precision of language signals quality
- No exclamation points
- No hyperbole (let the product speak)
- Elegant brevity
- Detail is selective — only details that matter to the discerning reader

### Vocabulary set
**Preferred:** crafted, curated, refined, considered, distinguished, precise, exceptional, rare, discerning, intentional, enduring, understated, reserved, singular
**Avoid:** amazing, awesome, incredible, best deal, limited time, don't miss out, huge

### Forbidden phrases
- "This is the best _____ you'll ever ___"
- Any sentence ending with an exclamation point
- "Affordable luxury" (contradiction in premium positioning)
- "Sale," "discount," "cheap" in premium context

### Example output
> We make twelve watches per year.
>
> Each requires 400 hours. Two craftsmen. One generation of knowledge passed between them before a single component is cut.
>
> The waitlist is three years. We do not advertise.
>
> This is the only announcement we will make this year.

## PROFILE 7: INTELLECTUAL / THOUGHT LEADER
*Nuanced, evidence-backed, original*

### Who uses this
Researchers, analysts, subject matter experts, academics writing for professional audiences, senior professionals establishing intellectual authority in their field.

### Characteristics
- Takes a position — not "on one hand, on the other hand" paralysis
- Supports positions with evidence, observation, or specific experience
- Acknowledges complexity without hiding behind it
- Rewrites conventional wisdom by presenting evidence or logic against it
- Dense but not heavy — every sentence earns its place

### Vocabulary set
**Preferred:** the data suggests, in practice, the evidence points to, the critical distinction, what most models miss, the counterintuitive finding, precisely because, the mechanism is, what this actually means
**Avoid:** arguably (weak hedge), I think we can all agree, obviously (condescending), as everyone knows

### Forbidden phrases
- "In today's complex world..." (vague framing)
- "Thought leadership" as a self-description
- "I've been thinking a lot about..." (just state the thought)

### Example output
> The conventional approach to sales forecasting treats pipeline as a probability — assign a percentage to each stage, multiply, aggregate. This produces a number that looks precise and is almost never correct.
>
> The better model: forecast by behavior, not stage. What has the prospect actually done? Not said — done. Scheduled a call is a stage. Joined a call with their CFO is a behavior.
>
> Three behavioral signals predict close probability more accurately than any stage-based model I've seen tested. The first two are obvious once named. The third surprises most teams.

## PROFILE 8: WARM PROFESSIONAL
*Approachable, genuine, credible*

### Who uses this
Professionals building authentic relationships, consultants and advisors, personal brands across most industries, and anyone whose professional differentiation is partly their personality and relatability.

### Characteristics
- Professional but not stiff
- Uses first person naturally and frequently
- Admits to learning, mistakes, and uncertainty where genuine
- Builds trust through honesty, not just credentials
- Conversational rhythm without being casual

### Vocabulary set
**Preferred:** what I've noticed, here's what happened, what changed for me, the thing I got wrong, what actually works, the conversation I keep having, in my experience, what nobody tells you
**Avoid:** hey guys, sup, lowkey, tbh (too casual), vibes (for professional contexts)

### Forbidden phrases
- "Not sure if this is just me, but..." (confidence erosion)
- "Rant incoming" (frames value negatively)
- "Nobody talks about this" (often untrue and signals hyperbole)

### Example output
> I gave the worst presentation of my career in front of 200 people in 2019.
>
> Not because I didn't know the material. I knew it cold. I failed because I spent 45 minutes on what I knew and 90 seconds on what they needed.
>
> Seven years later, that is still the most useful feedback I have ever received — and I received it from a colleague at dinner afterward, not from the audience.
>
> Here is what I do differently now.

## PROFILE 9: CRISIS / SENSITIVE
*Careful, accountable, clear*

### Who uses this
Crisis communications, apologies, responses to controversy, sensitive personnel announcements, organizational issues, and any communication where getting the tone wrong has amplified consequences.

### Characteristics
- Lead with acknowledgment, not defense
- Never minimize or deflect
- Clear about what is known and what is not yet known
- Accountable without being groveling
- Actions and next steps matter more than expressions of regret
- Shorter paragraphs — easy to read under stress

### Vocabulary set
**Preferred:** we understand, we take responsibility, we are committed, we have identified, we are addressing, we will, what happened, what we are doing, what comes next
**Avoid:** if anyone was offended (conditional apology), mistakes were made (passive accountability avoidance), to be clear (often precedes something that was unclear)

### Forbidden phrases
- "If anyone was offended..." — conditional apologies do not work
- "Mistakes were made..." — passive voice removes accountability
- "We pride ourselves on..." after something went wrong
- "This was an isolated incident" (before the investigation is complete)

### Example output
> What happened on [date] was a failure of our systems and our judgment. Both.
>
> We have identified what went wrong. We are not going to share speculation before the review is complete, but we will share findings when it is.
>
> The people affected deserve more than an explanation. They deserve action. Here is what we have already done, and what happens next.

## TONE SELECTION FLOWCHART

```
Is this an organizational crisis or sensitive issue?
└── YES → CRISIS/SENSITIVE

Is this from or attributed to a CEO/founder?
└── YES → CEO/FOUNDER

Is this a luxury or premium brand context?
└── YES → LUXURY/PREMIUM

Is this a formal regulatory or compliance communication?
└── YES → CORPORATE

Is this a position, analysis, or expertise demonstration?
└── YES → INTELLECTUAL/THOUGHT LEADER

Is this a leadership or team-facing communication?
└── YES → LEADERSHIP

Is this a cross-stakeholder or politically sensitive communication?
└── YES → DIPLOMATIC

Is this from a VP or director level?
└── YES → EXECUTIVE

No strong signal?
└── DEFAULT → WARM PROFESSIONAL
```
