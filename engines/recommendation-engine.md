## PURPOSE

The Recommendation Engine delivers strategic communication advice — what to post, when to post it, how to frame it, what platform to use, how to handle a difficult communication situation, and how to build long-term communication equity. It does not produce content; it produces the strategy that precedes content.

## ANGLE SELECTION FRAMEWORK

When a user knows what they want to talk about but not how, apply this framework:

### The Angle Test — Five Questions
1. **What does the audience already believe about this topic?**
   → Find the gap between their belief and a better truth

2. **What does everyone else in this space say about this topic?**
   → Find the angle that is not already saturated

3. **What is the most counterintuitive true thing about this topic?**
   → Counterintuitive + true = memorable

4. **What personal experience does the author have that is relevant?**
   → Specific experience > general observation every time

5. **What is the single most useful thing someone could know about this topic today?**
   → Utility + specificity = value

### Angle options ranked by impact
1. **Personal experience that contradicts the conventional view** — Highest impact; hardest to fake
2. **Data or evidence that surprises the audience** — High impact; requires sourcing
3. **A prediction or position with specific reasoning** — High impact; requires courage
4. **A reframe of a familiar idea** — Medium-high impact; requires originality
5. **A curated insight with commentary** — Medium impact; requires distinct perspective
6. **An explanation of something complex** — Medium impact; education-forward
7. **An announcement** — Variable impact; depends on what is announced

## PLATFORM SELECTION GUIDE

When a user has content and needs to know where to put it:

### Decision Matrix

| Content Type | Best Platform | Second Best |
|---|---|---|
| Thought leadership / expertise | LinkedIn | Medium/Substack |
| Visual product or lifestyle | Instagram | Pinterest |
| Short opinion or commentary | X/Twitter | LinkedIn |
| Long educational content | YouTube | LinkedIn article |
| Community building | Facebook Group | LinkedIn |
| Behind-the-scenes / authentic | TikTok | Instagram Stories |
| Professional announcement | LinkedIn | Press release |
| Personal milestone | LinkedIn | Instagram |
| Crisis communication | LinkedIn (primary) | Official website |
| Employee / team culture | LinkedIn | Instagram |
| Local business promotion | Facebook | Instagram |
| Technical tutorial | YouTube | LinkedIn article |
| Brand narrative | LinkedIn | YouTube |

### Repurposing Hierarchy

One long-form piece generates many derivative pieces:

```
LONG-FORM SOURCE (YouTube video, LinkedIn article, Podcast episode)
    ↓
MEDIUM-FORM (LinkedIn post summary, Instagram carousel)
    ↓
SHORT-FORM (X/Twitter thread, Instagram caption, TikTok script)
    ↓
MICRO-FORM (Single tweet, Instagram Story quote, WhatsApp status)
```

Maximum value from minimum effort: create long → distribute short.

## TIMING RECOMMENDATIONS

### LinkedIn
- Best days: Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday
- Best times: 7–9 AM (before work), 12–1 PM (lunch), 5–6 PM (commute)
- Avoid: Friday afternoon, Saturday, Sunday (engagement drops significantly)
- Cadence: 3–5 posts per week for authority-building; 1–2 per week minimum for presence

### Instagram
- Best days: Monday, Wednesday, Friday
- Best times: 11 AM–1 PM, 7–9 PM
- Cadence: 4–7 posts per week (feed + Stories); Stories daily for top-of-feed visibility

### X/Twitter
- Best times: 8–10 AM, 12–1 PM, 5–7 PM
- Cadence: 1–5 times per day for growth accounts; 3–5x per week for professional presence
- Threads: post any time; engagement sustains for 24–48 hours

### TikTok
- Best times: 6–10 AM, 7–11 PM
- Cadence: 1–3 per day for growth; minimum 3–4 per week for presence

### YouTube
- Best days: Thursday, Friday (publish so viewers watch over the weekend)
- Cadence: 1 video per week consistent beats 3 videos one week then silence

### General principle
Consistency > optimal timing. The best time to post is a time you can consistently show up.

## FRAMING FOR DIFFERENT AUDIENCES

### The C-suite audience
**What they want:** Clarity, brevity, conclusion first
**What they do not want:** Context they already have, emotional persuasion they did not ask for
**How to frame:** Problem → decision → outcome → next step
**What to avoid:** Long preambles, qualifications, overly personal stories

### The peer professional audience
**What they want:** Recognition of shared experience, specific insights, honest takes
**What they do not want:** Advice that talks down to them, generic tips they already know
**How to frame:** Shared context → specific observation → perspective → invitation to discuss
**What to avoid:** Presenting yourself as the authority on their experience

### The general professional audience
**What they want:** Accessible value, something useful, something they can share
**What they do not want:** Jargon, assumed context, insider references
**How to frame:** Universal observation → specific example → clear application
**What to avoid:** Assuming domain knowledge; assuming familiarity with your work

### The young professional audience
**What they want:** Honesty about how things actually work, not how they are supposed to work
**What they do not want:** Condescension, advice from a position of privilege that ignores their constraints
**How to frame:** Real experience → what I wish I'd known → specific action they can take
**What to avoid:** "When I was your age" framing; ignoring systemic constraints

## RISK/BENEFIT ANALYSIS FRAMEWORK

When advising on a communication decision with known risks, apply this framework:

### Step 1: Identify the risk category
- **Reputational risk** — Could this make the author look bad to any significant group?
- **Legal risk** — Could this create liability?
- **Relationship risk** — Could this damage a professional relationship?
- **Timing risk** — Is this the wrong moment to say this?
- **Platform risk** — Could this violate platform terms of service?

### Step 2: Assess probability and severity
- **Probability:** High / Medium / Low
- **Severity if realized:** Critical / Significant / Minor

### Step 3: Identify the upside
- What is the communication gain if this works?
- Who would respond positively and how many?
- What long-term equity does this build?

### Step 4: Provide the recommendation
```
RISK: [Specific risk identified]
PROBABILITY: [High/Medium/Low]
SEVERITY: [Critical/Significant/Minor]
UPSIDE: [Communication benefit]
RECOMMENDATION: [Proceed / Proceed with modification / Do not proceed]
ALTERNATIVE: [What to do instead if proceeding is not advised]
```

## GROWTH LEVER RECOMMENDATIONS

When a user wants to build platform presence over time, recommend the highest-leverage activities:

### LinkedIn authority-building levers (ranked)
1. Original point-of-view posts (your take on an industry issue) — highest signal
2. Specific personal experience posts — high trust
3. Data-backed insight posts — high credibility
4. Thoughtful responses to prominent posts in your field — visibility + relationship
5. LinkedIn articles for evergreen depth — SEO and reference value
6. Engagement with your own comments — community signal

### Instagram personal brand levers
1. Consistent aesthetic + consistent voice — foundational
2. Carousel posts (highest organic reach per post)
3. Reels with educational or entertaining hook — discovery engine
4. Stories daily — maintains top-of-feed position
5. Bio optimization — the first impression for new visitors

### Content growth principles (platform-agnostic)
1. The best growth strategy is publishing something worth sharing
2. Consistency compounds — a mediocre post published every week beats a brilliant post published once a quarter
3. Engagement creates reach — reply to every comment in the first hour after posting
4. Collaboration multiplies — featuring others exposes you to their audience
