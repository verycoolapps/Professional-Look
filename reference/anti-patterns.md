# ANTI-PATTERNS
## The Professional Communication Hall of Shame

**Module:** `reference/anti-patterns.md`
**Loaded by:** Content diagnosis, ANALYZE task class, anti-slop review
**Dependencies:** `engines/anti-ai-slop-engine.md`, `core/professional-standards.md`

## PURPOSE

This module documents failures — specific examples of professional communication that damages the author's credibility, dilutes their message, or does the opposite of what it intended. Each entry includes what went wrong, why it fails, and a corrected version.

Understanding failure patterns is as important as understanding best practices.

---

## CATEGORY 1: AI SLOP PATTERNS

### AP-01: The Hollow Opener

**The failure:**
> "In today's fast-paced, ever-changing business landscape, staying ahead of the curve is more important than ever. As professionals navigate these uncertain times, the need for adaptive strategies cannot be overstated."

**Why it fails:**
- Contains zero information
- Could have been written by anyone, about anything, at any time
- Signals that the writer had nothing to say before starting
- Screams: "AI generated this"

**Fixed:**
> "Last quarter, three of the five enterprise deals we lost came down to the same issue — one nobody in our post-mortems was willing to name directly."

---

### AP-02: The Enthusiasm Escalation

**The failure:**
> "I am beyond thrilled and incredibly honored to share that I have been absolutely blown away by the overwhelming response to our latest initiative! We are beyond grateful for every single one of you who has been part of this incredible journey!!"

**Why it fails:**
- Emotion substituting for content
- Triple exclamation points
- Words like "incredible" and "beyond" are filler
- Reads as performance, not communication

**Fixed:**
> "Our Q3 initiative reached 40,000 users in eight weeks — three times the number we planned for. The specific reason matters more than the number."

---

### AP-03: The Strategic Language Salad

**The failure:**
> "By leveraging synergistic partnerships and unlocking value-driven paradigm shifts, we are poised to disrupt the ecosystem and move the needle on our strategic objectives going forward."

**Why it fails:**
- Fourteen words of vocabulary; zero information
- Every term is a cliché
- No reader would be able to act on this
- Classic AI-pattern sentence: confident-sounding, content-free

**Fixed:**
> "We are partnering with [Company X] to integrate our platform into their logistics network — reducing customer onboarding from eight weeks to four."

---

### AP-04: The Learning Journey

**The failure:**
> "I wanted to share some reflections on my incredible learning journey over the past year. Every experience has taught me so much and helped me grow both personally and professionally."

**Why it fails:**
- Hollow generality — no specific learning identified
- "Incredible learning journey" is meaningless
- "Grown personally and professionally" — everyone says this; no one means anything specific by it

**Fixed:**
> "The most important thing I learned managing a product through a failed launch: what I tell the board in the first 24 hours matters more than the launch outcome itself."

---

### AP-05: The Advice Column Non-Advice

**The failure:**
> "Success isn't linear! It's okay to fail — that's how we grow. Remember: every setback is a setup for a comeback. Believe in yourself and the process!"

**Why it fails:**
- Pure motivational content with no information value
- No specific advice, no evidence, no application
- Could have been written by anyone who has seen a motivational poster

**Fixed:**
> "Two years ago I killed a product that had taken three years to build. Here is the single mistake in how I made that decision — and what I'd do differently."

## CATEGORY 2: CORPORATE SPEAK FAILURES

### AP-06: The Passive Accountability Void

**The failure:**
> "It has come to our attention that certain customers may have experienced service disruptions. Steps are being taken to address the situation. We apologize for any inconvenience this may have caused."

**Why it fails:**
- Passive voice removes accountability entirely
- "May have experienced" — they either did or did not
- "Certain customers" — how many? Which ones?
- "Any inconvenience" minimizes what may have been a significant problem

**Fixed:**
> "A database failure on [date] disrupted service for 2,400 customers for 6 hours. We know what caused it. We have fixed it. Here is what we owe each affected account and what we are doing to prevent recurrence."

---

### AP-07: The Circle Back Request

**The failure:**
> "Thanks for your email! I'd love to connect on this. Let me circle back to you next week when I've had a chance to touch base with the relevant stakeholders and get some bandwidth to deep dive into the specifics."

**Why it fails:**
- "Circle back," "touch base," "bandwidth," "deep dive" — four clichés in one sentence
- Vague timeline ("next week") with no commitment
- "Relevant stakeholders" — who?
- Delays action while performing action

**Fixed:**
> "I'll review this with Sarah (Engineering) and Tom (Finance) by Thursday and respond by end of day Friday."

---

### AP-08: The Strategic Initiative Announcement

**The failure:**
> "We are proud to announce the launch of our transformative strategic initiative designed to leverage our core competencies and deliver world-class solutions to our valued customers."

**Why it fails:**
- "Transformative strategic initiative" — meaningless
- "Core competencies" — every company has these
- "World-class solutions" — evidence required
- "Valued customers" — corporate boilerplate that sounds the opposite of genuine

**Fixed:**
> "We are launching [Initiative Name] — a new pricing structure that eliminates the three tiers our customers told us were confusing and replaces them with one. Details below."

## CATEGORY 3: HUMBLE-BRAG GONE WRONG

### AP-09: The Grateful Over-Sharer

**The failure:**
> "I am just so incredibly humbled and overwhelmed by the response to my recent promotion to Partner! I honestly don't know what I did to deserve such an incredible team, such amazing mentors, and such a beautiful life. God is so good!!! #blessed"

**Why it fails:**
- Performs humility while actually broadcasting achievement
- Audience sees through it; the performer usually does not
- Religion and blessing language in professional contexts risks alienating a secular or multi-faith audience
- The exclamation points undermine the "humbled" claim

**Fixed:**
> "Made Partner today.
>
> The five people most responsible for that outcome did not get the title. They got the work done while I learned how to lead.
>
> I won't make the same mistake in the next chapter."

---

### AP-10: The Declined Opportunity Flex

**The failure:**
> "Grateful to have been approached about several major opportunities recently, including a significant C-suite role that I ultimately decided wasn't the right fit. Sometimes the best decisions are the ones you don't make. #leadership #authenticity"

**Why it fails:**
- Disguised as humility; actually just bragging about being recruited
- Provides no value to the reader
- "Sometimes the best decisions are the ones you don't make" — hollow wisdom
- Hashtags amplify the inauthenticity

**Fixed:** Do not post this. If there is a genuine decision-making insight here, write about the decision-making framework itself — not the fact that you had to use it.

## CATEGORY 4: ENGAGEMENT BAIT

### AP-11: The Artificial List Hook

**The failure:**
> "10 things successful people do every morning that you're probably not doing 🧵👇"

**Why it fails:**
- Promises specific insight; almost always delivers generic advice
- The format has been used so many times it generates automatic skepticism
- "That you're probably not doing" is mildly condescending

**Fixed:**
> "I have worked with 40+ CEOs on their morning routines. Two of them do the same three things. The rest do whatever they want. Here is what the two have in common that might actually matter."

---

### AP-12: The Manufactured Vulnerability

**The failure:**
> "Let me be real with you guys. Building a business is HARD. Some days I question everything. But then I remember my why and I keep going. Drop a ❤️ if you can relate!"

**Why it fails:**
- Performs vulnerability without being vulnerable (no specific situation, no real admission)
- "My why" is a cliché that substitutes for actual reflection
- Requests engagement before delivering value

**Fixed:**
> "Last year, I spent three months convinced we were building the wrong product. I was right. Here is what the decision to change it cost, and why I would make it again."

## CATEGORY 5: TONE FAILURES

### AP-13: The Condescending Tutorial

**The failure:**
> "A lot of professionals don't understand the basics of compound interest. So let me break it down in a simple way that everyone can understand. Basically, it means you earn interest on your interest!"

**Why it fails:**
- The audience (LinkedIn professionals) knows what compound interest is
- "Let me break it down in a simple way" signals condescension
- Teaching the audience something they already know destroys credibility

**Fixed:** Lead with the non-obvious application or implication of compound interest, not the definition. Assume the audience's intelligence.

---

### AP-14: The Overly Casual Executive

**The failure:**
> "Hey LinkedIn! So excited to share our Q3 results! We totally crushed it lol! Honestly couldn't be more pumped about where we're headed!!"

**Why it fails:**
- Register mismatch: "lol" and "pumped" in a business performance context
- "Totally crushed it" is aspirational language without evidence
- The exclamation pattern communicates low effort

**Fixed:**
> "Q3: $4.2M in revenue, 28% year-over-year growth, and a product launch that exceeded every benchmark we set.
>
> The number I care about most is not on this list. I'll share it in the next post."

---

### AP-15: The Defensive Public Complaint

**The failure:**
> "PSA: If you reach out to someone asking for a favor and they help you, at least have the courtesy to follow up. I'm constantly going above and beyond for people in my network who never bother to say thank you. Know your worth."

**Why it fails:**
- Airing grievances publicly damages the author's image more than any recipient's
- "Know your worth" is directed at the writer, not the offenders
- Invites the audience to wonder if the author is difficult to work with

**Fixed:** Do not post this. Address the specific relationship issue privately, or use the experience to write something genuinely useful about building professional relationships.

## CATEGORY 6: CULTURAL INSENSITIVITY

### AP-16: The Universal Assumption

**The failure:**
> "Happy Thanksgiving from our team to yours! We hope you're spending the day with family and enjoying the holiday!"

When posted to an international audience.

**Why it fails:**
- Assumes a US-specific holiday applies to a global audience
- Non-US audiences feel excluded or confused
- For a professional brand with international reach, this signals lack of cultural awareness

**Fixed:** Either create platform-specific regional content, or reference the value (gratitude, family) without the specific cultural marker: "This week, our US team takes time to reflect on the year. Here is what we are grateful for."

## CATEGORY 7: REPUTATION RISKS

### AP-17: The Premature Victory Lap

**The failure:**
> "Excited to announce that we've just signed a term sheet with [Major Investor]! We're officially going to change the way this industry works!"

**Why it fails:**
- Term sheets are not closed deals; the deal can still fall through
- "Change the way this industry works" is a promise
- If the deal falls through, this post exists permanently

**Fixed:** Announce when the deal closes. Before that, publish nothing.

---

### AP-18: The Salary Post

**The failure:**
> "After my first year as a senior engineer, I'm proud to say I now earn $[specific amount]. If you're not negotiating, you're leaving money on the table."

**Why it fails:**
- May violate employment contract
- Creates comparison dynamics with peers that have unintended consequences
- The advice is valid but the execution risks the relationship with the employer

**Fixed:** Share the insight about negotiation without the specific salary figure. The insight is the value; the salary creates unnecessary risk.
