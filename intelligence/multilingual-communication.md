## PURPOSE

## ENGLISH: PROFESSIONAL REGISTER

### Business English — Core Principles

**Clarity over complexity:**
American and international business English prizes directness. The clearest sentence is almost always the best sentence. Complexity that does not add meaning is a signal of poor thinking, not sophistication.

**American vs. British conventions:**
| Feature | American | British |
|---|---|---|
| Spelling | optimize, analyze, program | optimise, analyse, programme |
| Date format | Month/Day/Year | Day/Month/Year |
| Quotation marks | "double" then 'single' | 'single' then "double" |
| Collective nouns | Company is (singular) | Company are (plural) |
| Formal close | Sincerely, Regards, | Kind regards, Yours sincerely, |

**Default for international professional communication:** American English, unless the specific context or client requires British.

### Register Calibration in English

**Formal register** (regulatory, legal, board):
- No contractions
- Full sentences; no fragments
- Third person for organizational communications
- Passive voice acceptable in formal announcements

**Semi-formal register** (executive email, professional posts):
- Contractions acceptable: "we're," "it's," "I've"
- Active voice preferred
- First person preferred
- Structured but not stiff

**Professional casual** (TikTok, Instagram, community posts):
- Full contractions
- Conversational sentences and fragments
- "And" and "But" can start sentences
- Short punchy rhythm

## INDONESIAN PROFESSIONAL COMMUNICATION

### Overview: The Professional Bahasa Indonesia Context

Indonesian professional communication operates within a cultural context that differs significantly from the direct, transactional register of American business English. Understanding the cultural substrate is as important as understanding the language.

**Key cultural dimensions affecting communication:**

**1. Collectivist orientation**
Indonesians operate within collective identities — family, organization, community — and communication reflects this. Achievements are often framed as collective (kami/kita) rather than purely individual. Taking all-individual credit for a team achievement reads as arrogant.

**2. Hierarchical respect**
Professional hierarchy is respected and reflected in language. Speaking to or about someone senior requires appropriate register. The distinction between informal you (kamu) and formal you (Anda, Bapak/Ibu) is not interchangeable.

**3. Indirect communication for sensitive topics**
Directness is valued in professional Bahasa — but not for negative feedback, disagreement, or criticism. These are typically softened, embedded in positive framing, or communicated through implication. A direct "this is wrong" in Indonesian professional context lands differently than in English; the preferred form is "mungkin bisa dipertimbangkan alternatif lain" (perhaps another alternative could be considered).

**4. Relationship-first**
Indonesian professional culture values relationship before transaction. An email that begins with the request without relational context feels abrupt. Small relational openings — a reference to a previous meeting, an acknowledgment of the person's time, a polite opening — are not filler; they are part of the communication.

**5. Modesty norm**
Explicit self-promotion in the Western LinkedIn style can feel culturally uncomfortable in Indonesian professional context. Achievement is better framed with team credit and expressed through outcomes rather than individual superlatives.

### Formal Bahasa Indonesia vs. Modern Professional Bahasa

**Formal Bahasa (most written official, governmental, corporate)**
- Full formal grammar
- Consistent use of Anda (not kamu)
- Bapak/Ibu for direct address
- No loan words where Indonesian equivalents exist
- Passive constructions common: "telah dilakukan," "akan disampaikan"

**Modern Professional Bahasa (most LinkedIn, email, professional social)**
- Clear, readable, educated
- Anda for formal address; some flexibility in established relationships
- Selective use of English loan words that are industry-standard (inovasi, strategi, tim, platform, konten)
- Code-switching acceptable for terms with no clean Indonesian equivalent
- First person (saya) more common than full passive

**Informal/Social Bahasa (Instagram, TikTok captions)**
- Gue/aku depending on the speaker's region and age (Jakarta: gue; national: aku)
- Heavy code-switching (mix of English and Indonesian natural in urban professional context)
- Abbreviated forms common
- Direct, punchy

### Code-Switching Guidance

Indonesian professional content — especially in tech, startup, and creative industries — naturally mixes Indonesian and English. This is not a failure of language; it is the authentic register of urban Indonesian professionals.

**When code-switching is appropriate:**
- Technical terms with no clean Indonesian equivalent (software, pitch deck, platform, content creator, startup, fundraising)
- English terms the Indonesian professional audience expects in that context
- Brand names and industry-specific proper nouns

**When to use Indonesian:**
- When writing for a government, education, or formal institutional audience
- When the content's purpose is to be accessible to a broader Indonesian audience
- When the platform or context expects Indonesian (formal business letters, official announcements)

**When to avoid code-switching:**
- When it reads as affected or performative rather than natural
- When the Indonesian term is equal in clarity and the audience will recognize it
- In formal or regulatory contexts where Indonesian language standards apply

### Translation Philosophy

**Meaning-first, never word-for-word**

The test of a good translation is not whether it uses equivalent words — it is whether a native speaker of the target language would recognize it as something they would say in a professional context.

**Example:**
English: "Let me be clear — this decision was the right call."
Word-for-word: "Biarkan saya jelas — keputusan ini adalah pilihan yang tepat."
Professional Bahasa: "Izinkan saya menegaskan — ini adalah keputusan yang tepat."

**Idiom adaptation:**
Idioms do not translate. They must be adapted to an equivalent idiom in the target language or replaced with a clear statement of the meaning.

English: "Hit the ground running"
Word-for-word: (no direct equivalent; "memukul tanah sambil berlari" is meaningless)
Professional Bahasa: "Langsung tancap gas dari hari pertama" (informal) or "Bergerak cepat sejak awal" (formal)

### Indonesian Professional Vocabulary

**Authority terms:**
- kepemimpinan (leadership)
- eksekutif (executive)
- strategi (strategy)
- dampak (impact)
- kinerja (performance)
- transformasi (transformation)
- inovasi (innovation)

**Warm professional openers:**
- "Saya berharap pesan ini menemukan Anda dalam keadaan baik." (formal — I hope this message finds you well)
- "Terima kasih telah meluangkan waktu untuk membaca ini." (Thank you for taking the time to read this)
- "Saya ingin berbagi perspektif..." (I would like to share a perspective...)

**Achievement language (modest framing):**
- "Alhamdulillah, bersama tim kami berhasil mencapai..." (With gratitude, together with our team we achieved...)
- "Kami bersyukur bisa..." (We are grateful to be able to...)
- "Ini adalah hasil kerja bersama..." (This is the result of collaborative work...)

**Professional closings:**
- Formal: "Hormat kami," (Respectfully,) / "Salam hormat," (With respect,)
- Semi-formal: "Salam," (Regards,) / "Terima kasih," (Thank you,)
- Digital/social: "Salam hangat," (Warm regards,)

### Localization vs. Globalization

**Localization:** Adapting content to feel native to the Indonesian professional audience. Appropriate for: content targeting Indonesian-specific audiences, domestic business communication, community-facing social content.

**Globalization:** Creating content accessible to both Indonesian and international audiences. Appropriate for: content reaching both English and Indonesian speakers, international platforms where Indonesian context is the differentiating element.

**When localizing Indonesian content:**
- Reference Indonesian professional norms and context where relevant
- Use unit references appropriate to Indonesia (Rupiah, not USD, unless context is international)
- Calendar and seasonal references appropriate to Indonesian context
- Acknowledge regional diversity where relevant

## ADAPTATION QUALITY CHECK

Before delivering any translated or adapted content:

- [ ] Does this read naturally in the target language?
- [ ] Are register and formality appropriate for the audience and context?
- [ ] Have all idioms been adapted (not word-translated)?
- [ ] Are there any false-cognate risks? (English words that look like Indonesian words but mean different things)
- [ ] Does the cultural register match the professional context?
- [ ] Are relational framing elements present where the culture expects them?
