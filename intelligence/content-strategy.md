## PURPOSE

Content strategy is the system that makes individual pieces of content more valuable than the sum of their parts. Without strategy, content is random. With strategy, it is compounding.

This module provides the full framework for building, executing, and adjusting a professional content strategy across platforms and over time.

## CONTENT PILLARS FRAMEWORK

Content pillars are the 3–5 thematic territories a professional or brand consistently communicates within. They are the categories that define what you talk about and — equally important — what you do not.

### The Six Pillar Types

**1. Authority Pillar**
Your core area of professional expertise. The territory where you have the most to say and the most credibility to say it.
- Content type: frameworks, analysis, research, opinion
- Purpose: positions you as the expert in this space
- Example for a product leader: "Product strategy in fast-growth companies"

**2. Human Pillar**
The personal dimension that makes your expertise trustworthy and your brand relatable.
- Content type: honest reflection, personal stories, lessons from failure
- Purpose: builds trust through vulnerability and specificity
- Example: "What managing a team through a layoff actually felt like"

**3. Behind-the-Scenes Pillar**
The reality of how your work is done — the process, the tools, the decisions others do not see.
- Content type: day-in-the-life, decision documentation, how you solve specific problems
- Purpose: creates aspirational access and demonstrates expertise through process
- Example: "How I structure my first 90 days in a new executive role"

**4. Educational Pillar**
Transferable knowledge your audience can apply.
- Content type: how-to guides, frameworks, tool recommendations, explainers
- Purpose: provides immediate value; earns saves and shares
- Example: "A framework for running product reviews that don't waste everyone's time"

**5. Opinion/Perspective Pillar**
Your distinctive take on what is happening in your field.
- Content type: commentary on industry events, predictions, counterintuitive positions
- Purpose: differentiates you from people who only share information; demonstrates thinking
- Example: "Why I think the AI productivity obsession is making companies less creative, not more"

**6. Social Proof Pillar**
Evidence that your work, advice, or approach produces results.
- Content type: client or colleague stories (with permission), quantified outcomes, public recognition
- Purpose: completes the credibility equation
- Example: "A client applied this framework to their Q3 planning. Here's what changed."

### Pillar Balance

| Pillar Type | Content Mix Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Authority | 25–30% |
| Human | 15–20% |
| Educational | 20–25% |
| Opinion/Perspective | 15–20% |
| Behind-the-Scenes | 5–10% |
| Social Proof | 5–10% |

## EDITORIAL CALENDAR LOGIC

An editorial calendar is not a rigid production schedule — it is a framework that prevents random content creation and ensures pillar balance over time.

### Monthly Planning Structure

**Week 1:** Map topics for the month (one per pillar)
**Week 2:** Identify the angles and hooks for each topic
**Week 3:** Produce and schedule ahead of the week's posts
**Week 4:** Review what worked, adjust for next month

### Topic Mapping Process

Step 1: What happened in your professional world this month that is relevant to your audience?
Step 2: What is being discussed in your field that you have a genuine perspective on?
Step 3: What has your audience been asking you about?
Step 4: What are you learning right now that others in your position would want to know?
Step 5: What results or outcomes occurred that you can document (with appropriate permission)?

These five questions generate 15–20 topic options per month. Select the 8–12 that have the strongest angles and fit your pillar balance.

## PLATFORM CROSS-POSTING STRATEGY

One piece of content should rarely be posted identically on every platform. The repurposing hierarchy:

### The Content Hierarchy

```
LONG-FORM SOURCE
(YouTube video / Podcast / LinkedIn Article / Newsletter)
    │
    ├── LinkedIn Post (key insight + one example, 150–300 words)
    │       └── LinkedIn Comment on your own post (one addition for the thread)
    │
    ├── X/Twitter Thread (same insight structured as 5–8 tweets)
    │       └── Single tweet (the most quotable line)
    │
    ├── Instagram Carousel (the framework, visual format)
    │       └── Instagram Reel (60s video version of the hook + insight)
    │       └── Instagram Story (poll or question derived from the content)
    │
    └── TikTok (the hook + one key point; 30–60s)
            └── Caption (3-line summary + hashtags)
```

### Platform Adaptation Rules

Each platform repurpose must:
1. Be written for the platform's native register (not just copy-pasted)
2. Stand alone without the original (viewers may see only this version)
3. Adapt in length to the platform's optimal range
4. Include platform-specific elements (hashtags, threads format, etc.)

## CONTENT FREQUENCY BY PLATFORM

| Platform | Minimum for Presence | Growth Rate |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | 2×/week | 4–5×/week |
| Instagram (Feed) | 3×/week | 5–7×/week |
| Instagram (Stories) | 3×/week | Daily |
| X/Twitter | 3×/week | Daily |
| TikTok | 3×/week | 1–3×/day |
| YouTube | 1×/2 weeks | 1×/week |
| Facebook | 2×/week | 4×/week |
| Newsletter | 1×/month | 1–2×/week |

**The most important frequency rule:** Consistency beats cadence. A creator who posts three times per week, every week, without fail, outperforms a creator who posts five times per week for one month and then goes dark for three.

## EVERGREEN vs. TIMELY CONTENT

### Evergreen content
Content that remains relevant for months or years after publication. The backbone of long-term content strategy.

**Characteristics:**
- Addresses a persistent problem or question in the field
- Not tied to a current event or trend
- Can be referenced and shared long after publication

**Examples:** "How to structure a product review," "The five conversations every new manager must have," "What I wish I'd known before my first C-suite role"

**Strategy:** 60–70% of content should be evergreen. It compounds over time — a good evergreen post from six months ago can still be driving engagement today.

### Timely content
Content tied to a current event, trend, or industry development. Higher initial engagement ceiling but shorter shelf life.

**Characteristics:**
- Relevant for days to weeks after publication
- Often receives higher initial engagement (tied to trending conversation)
- Positions you as current and plugged in

**Examples:** Reactions to industry news, commentary on recent research, responses to a viral trend in your space

**Strategy:** 30–40% of content. Provides spikes in reach; builds perception of being current.

## ANALYTICS INTERPRETATION

### Metrics that matter (and what they mean)

| Metric | What It Signals | Platform |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions | Reach — how many people saw this | All |
| Engagement rate | Quality — what % of viewers engaged | All |
| Saves | High-value content — worth keeping | Instagram, LinkedIn |
| Shares/Reposts | Highest signal — people valued this enough to share | All |
| Comments | Conversation — did this prompt thinking? | All |
| Profile visits | Audience intent — interested enough to learn more | All |
| Follower growth | Long-term trust accumulation | All |
| Watch time / Completion rate | Content quality — did people stay? | YouTube, TikTok |

### What low engagement means
Low engagement on a single post means little. Low engagement across 10+ posts means something. Look for patterns:
- Low engagement across all posts → Hook problem or audience-content mismatch
- Low engagement on one content type → That content type does not work for this audience
- Declining engagement over time → Content has become predictable; introduce new angles

## GROWTH PLATEAU SOLUTIONS

When consistent posting is not producing growth, the problem is usually one of these:

**Problem 1: The content is good but not shareable**
Fix: Add more counterintuitive angles; add more specific data; make the structure more obviously shareable (lists, frameworks, carousels)

**Problem 2: The content is consistent but not distinctive**
Fix: Audit competitors and find the territory they are not occupying; differentiate angle, not just topic

**Problem 3: The audience is saturated**
Fix: Introduce collaborations — appear on others' platforms; create content that introduces you to adjacent audiences

**Problem 4: The platform algorithm has changed**
Fix: Experiment with format rather than topic; the platform may be favoring a different content type (e.g., video over text)

**Problem 5: The niche is too narrow**
Fix: Slightly widen the pillar to attract adjacent audiences while maintaining the core expertise differentiation
