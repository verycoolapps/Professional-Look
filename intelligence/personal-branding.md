## PURPOSE

A personal brand is not a personal marketing campaign. It is the coherent, consistent, and deliberate expression of who you are and what you stand for in professional contexts. It is built through the accumulation of hundreds of individual communications — each one either reinforcing or diluting the overall impression.

This module provides the full framework for building, maintaining, and protecting a professional personal brand.

## BRAND IDENTITY FRAMEWORK

### Step 1: Define Your Positioning

The positioning statement answers: who are you, for whom, and what is different about the value you provide?

**Formula:**
```
For [specific professional audience],
I am [role/expertise],
who [delivers specific outcome],
unlike [alternative options],
because [unique differentiator — experience, method, perspective, or access].
```

**Example (Career Coach):**
> For senior professionals navigating executive transitions,
> I am a career strategist
> who reduces the time to landing by 40% on average through positioning — not job boards,
> unlike traditional career coaches who focus on résumé polish,
> because I have personally hired at VP and C-level at six organizations across three industries.

**Common positioning mistakes:**
- Positioning for everyone (reaches no one)
- Positioning based on credentials instead of outcomes
- Positioning that sounds identical to competitors
- Positioning that the person cannot credibly own

---

### Step 2: Identify Your Brand Pillars

Brand pillars are the 3–5 core themes you consistently communicate. They are the territories you own in your audience's mind. Every piece of content you create touches at least one of them.

**Pillar selection criteria:**
1. Topics you know more about than most people in your network
2. Topics your target audience cares about and needs
3. Topics that differentiate you from others in your space
4. Topics you can sustain content about indefinitely

**Pillar types:**
- **Expertise pillar** — Core professional knowledge (e.g., "Enterprise SaaS sales strategy")
- **Experience pillar** — Hard-won lessons from your actual history (e.g., "What building a company in a downturn actually looks like")
- **Perspective pillar** — Your distinct point of view on your field (e.g., "Why most leadership advice ignores what actually motivates people")
- **Human pillar** — The personal dimension that makes expertise trustworthy (e.g., "What being a parent changed about how I lead")
- **Future pillar** — Where you see your field going (e.g., "AI will make execution skills irrelevant; judgment is the career investment now")

**Why pillars matter:**
Without pillars, content becomes random. Random content cannot build brand equity because the audience cannot form a consistent impression. Consistency of theme, repeated across many pieces over time, is how the audience comes to associate the brand with a specific idea.

### Step 3: Define Your Tone of Voice

Brand voice is not the tone profile from `engines/tone-intelligence.md` alone — it is the consistent expression of personality across all content. Two professionals with identical credentials and the same Warm Professional tone profile can have entirely different brand voices.

**Voice dimensions:**
- **Vocabulary** — Do you use industry terms or translate for a general audience?
- **Sentence structure** — Short and punchy or longer and analytical?
- **Use of humor** — Dry wit, warm humor, or no humor?
- **Personal disclosure** — How much of your life outside work appears in your content?
- **Opinion strength** — Do you take strong public positions, or do you moderate?

**Voice document structure:**
```
OUR VOICE IS: [3–5 adjectives that describe the brand voice]
OUR VOICE IS NOT: [3–5 contrasting qualities to avoid]
WE SAY: [Examples of language this brand uses]
WE DON'T SAY: [Examples of language this brand avoids]
SIGNATURE PHRASES: [Any recurring language that is distinctive]
```

### Step 4: Define Your Content Archetypes

Content archetypes are the recurring types of content you create. Having defined archetypes prevents content from becoming random and makes planning sustainable.

**Common professional content archetypes:**

1. **The Insight Post** — A specific professional observation with evidence
2. **The Story Post** — A personal or professional experience with a clear lesson
3. **The Framework Post** — A structured mental model or decision framework
4. **The Counterintuitive Post** — A claim that contradicts conventional wisdom, with evidence
5. **The Behind-the-Scenes Post** — How something actually works in practice
6. **The Milestone Post** — Professional achievements communicated with context and humility
7. **The Curation Post** — Resources, books, or ideas with distinctive commentary
8. **The Question Post** — A genuine question to the audience that invites conversation

**Ratio recommendation:** Not every post should be the same archetype. A healthy mix:
- 30% Insight Posts (authority)
- 25% Story Posts (trust)
- 20% Framework/Educational Posts (value)
- 15% Counterintuitive Posts (engagement)
- 10% other (Milestone, Human, Question)

## REPUTATION EQUITY

Reputation equity is the accumulated trust and credibility that makes your communication more believable, your network more responsive, and your opportunities more available.

### What builds reputation equity
1. **Consistent demonstration of expertise** — Not claims; demonstrations
2. **Accurate predictions and positions** — Track record of being right (or honest about being wrong)
3. **Delivered promises** — Saying you will do something and doing it
4. **Generosity** — Sharing knowledge, connections, and credit without expectation
5. **Consistency over time** — Showing up for months and years, not just sprints

### What destroys reputation equity
1. **Inconsistency between public persona and behavior** — The fastest brand destroyer
2. **Overclaiming** — One unverifiable claim undermines all verified ones
3. **Disappearing and reappearing** — Audiences do not maintain waiting positions for brands that go dark
4. **Reacting publicly to criticism** — Defensiveness signals insecurity; confident brands respond or ignore, not attack
5. **Content that is clearly not who you are** — Audiences sense inauthenticity and disengage

## PERSONAL TAGLINE

The personal tagline is a one-sentence expression of your positioning and value, usable across bio fields, presentations, and introductions.

**Formula:** [Role] + [Specific outcome] + [Differentiator]

**Examples:**
- "I help enterprise sales teams cut their average sales cycle from 6 months to 90 days."
- "CFO turned startup advisor — I specialize in the financial decisions that determine whether companies survive their first capital raise."
- "The person your executive team calls when they need to communicate the uncomfortably honest thing."

**Anti-patterns:**
- "I'm passionate about helping people reach their potential." (Generic; meaningless)
- "Entrepreneur | Speaker | Author | Coach." (Credential list, not value proposition)
- "Building the future of [industry]." (AI-pattern phrase; no information content)

## BRAND STORY ARC

Every strong personal brand has a narrative arc — a coherent story of how you came to know what you know and why it matters.

**The brand story structure:**
```
THE BEFORE: What the professional landscape looked like without your insight
THE DISCOVERY: The experience or realization that gave you your distinctive perspective
THE PROOF: Specific evidence that your approach works
THE MISSION: What you are doing with this knowledge for your audience
```

This arc does not need to be told in one post. It lives across multiple pieces and informs every piece, so that the audience who follows you for months gradually understands the full story.

## AUTHORITY SIGNALS

Authority is demonstrated, not declared. The strongest authority signals:

1. **Specificity** — People who have done the work can be specific; people who have only read about it cannot
2. **Track record** — Named outcomes, measurable results, specific companies and roles
3. **Being cited** — Others reference your work; you are quoted in media
4. **Access** — You talk to people, organizations, and sources that others do not
5. **Prediction accuracy** — You said something would happen; it did
6. **Independence** — You say things others in your field avoid saying (carefully, with evidence)

## TRUST-BUILDING CADENCE

Trust is built through consistent, repeated exposure over time. The research on trust formation: it takes approximately seven meaningful touchpoints before a professional relationship feels familiar. Twenty or more before genuine trust is established.

**Cadence implications:**
- Posting once and disappearing does not build trust
- Posting consistently for three months begins to build recognition
- Posting consistently for a year begins to build genuine brand equity
- The first six months of consistent content creation should be measured by learning and consistency, not results

---

## WHAT DESTROYS A PERSONAL BRAND

**Sudden register shifts:** An executive who posts thoughtful professional content and then posts something emotional, personal, or political that surprises the audience.

**Inconsistency of quality:** Ten excellent posts followed by five mediocre ones. The audience's trust drops faster than it was built.

**The deleted post:** Posting something and then deleting it when it receives negative attention. Screenshots exist. The delete is worse than the post.

**The public fight:** Arguing with critics publicly. Confident brands do not debate critics on their critics' terms.

**The overclaim caught:** One verifiable falsehood — one credential slightly inflated, one statistic invented — and all previous credibility is discounted.
