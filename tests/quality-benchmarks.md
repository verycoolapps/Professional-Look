## PURPOSE

This module provides concrete examples of content at each quality tier, enabling calibrated scoring. Use these benchmarks to train accurate quality assessment and to validate that the scoring rubric in `core/quality-control.md` is being applied consistently.

---

## SCORING FORMULA

Total score = Sum of seven dimension scores (maximum 100)

| Dimension | Max Points |
|---|---|
| Clarity | 15 |
| Tone Fit | 15 |
| Platform Fit | 15 |
| Authenticity | 15 |
| Impact | 15 |
| Originality | 15 |
| Reputation Safety | 10 |
| **TOTAL** | **100** |

---

## GRADE TIERS

| Tier | Score Range | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| ELITE | 90–100 | Deliver as-is |
| PROFESSIONAL | 80–89 | Deliver; note minor opportunity |
| ACCEPTABLE | 70–79 | Revise before delivering (below threshold) |
| NEEDS WORK | 60–69 | Significant revision required |
| REJECT | < 60 | Do not deliver; rebuild from different angle |

**Minimum delivery threshold: 85**

## TIER 1: ELITE (90–100) — Benchmark Examples

### Example 1A — LinkedIn Post (Score: 94)

> Nobody told me that the hardest part of scaling a company is not hiring fast enough.
>
> It's figuring out which decisions stop being yours to make.
>
> In 2022, I was still approving vendor contracts under $10K. I had 140 people. The bottleneck was not workload — it was me not trusting the systems we had built.
>
> The shift came from one question a board member asked: "What would break if you were unavailable for two weeks?"
>
> The answer changed every operational decision I made for the next six months.

**Scores:**
- Clarity: 14/15 — message is immediately clear
- Tone Fit: 14/15 — CEO/Founder, consistent throughout
- Platform Fit: 14/15 — LinkedIn-native format, appropriate length
- Authenticity: 15/15 — specific year, specific number, specific question quoted
- Impact: 14/15 — earns and holds the read; leaves a useful question for the reader
- Originality: 15/15 — specific angle; not a generic "learn to delegate" post
- Reputation Safety: 8/10 — no risks; safe

**Total: 94/100**

**What makes this ELITE:**
- The opening contradiction ("hardest part is NOT X") is a proven hook that immediately earns attention
- Specific: 2022, 140 people, $10K, two-week hypothetical
- The board member's question is quotable and useful — it does the audience a service
- No forbidden phrases; no hollow enthusiasm; no filler

### Example 1B — Crisis Statement (Score: 91)

> On November 2nd, a misconfiguration in our payment processing system caused 3,400 transactions to fail without notification to affected users. The failure lasted 11 hours.
>
> We identified the issue on November 3rd through a customer report, not our own monitoring. That is on us.
>
> Every affected account has been credited. We have sent direct notification to each one.
>
> The root cause was a configuration change deployed without adequate review. We have added a mandatory secondary review gate. The process now requires two engineers to approve any payment configuration change.
>
> We will publish a technical post-mortem by November 15th. If you were affected and have not received a credit, contact us directly at [email].

**Scores:**
- Clarity: 15/15 — what happened, when, to how many, for how long
- Tone Fit: 14/15 — Crisis/Sensitive throughout; accountable
- Platform Fit: 14/15 — appropriate for corporate website/LinkedIn; clear paragraphs
- Authenticity: 14/15 — specific dates, specific numbers, specific cause; no corporate-speak
- Impact: 14/15 — addresses all stakeholder questions; provides resolution
- Originality: 12/15 — well-executed crisis template; not innovative but not needed to be
- Reputation Safety: 8/10 — strong; appropriate disclosure level; no legal overclaiming

**Total: 91/100**

## TIER 2: PROFESSIONAL (80–89) — Benchmark Examples

### Example 2A — LinkedIn Post (Score: 84)

> Most career advice focuses on what to do next.
>
> The more useful question is: what to stop doing.
>
> After ten years managing product teams, the highest-leverage change I made was eliminating one meeting type that was consuming 40% of my calendar. Output went up. Team satisfaction scores went up.
>
> The meeting was not creating alignment. It was creating the appearance of alignment.

**Scores:**
- Clarity: 13/15 — clear message; slightly buried
- Tone Fit: 13/15 — Intellectual, mostly consistent; last sentence slightly academic
- Platform Fit: 13/15 — LinkedIn-native; appropriate length
- Authenticity: 12/15 — specific time and percentages; meeting type not named (slightly vague)
- Impact: 11/15 — useful but the reader is not told what to do with this; slightly incomplete
- Originality: 13/15 — reasonable angle; not overdone
- Reputation Safety: 9/10 — safe

**Total: 84/100**

**What keeps this PROFESSIONAL rather than ELITE:**
- The meeting type is not named — "what kind of meeting?" reduces specificity
- The close is an observation, not a landing — a reader wants to know what to replace it with
- "Creating the appearance of alignment" is good; the post earns more from that line than it delivers

- Originality: 12/15 — before/after format is common; execution is clean but not exceptional

## TIER 3: ACCEPTABLE (70–79) — Examples Below Threshold

### Example 3A — LinkedIn Post (Score: 74)

> Leadership is more than just managing people. It's about inspiring them to reach their full potential and helping them grow.
>
> Great leaders create environments where people feel safe to take risks and learn from their mistakes.
>
> I've been fortunate to work with some incredible leaders who have taught me so much. I try to bring those lessons to everything I do.
>
> What qualities do you think define a great leader? Let me know in the comments!

**Scores:**
- Clarity: 11/15 — message is present but generic
- Tone Fit: 11/15 — Warm Professional baseline; inconsistent warmth
- Platform Fit: 12/15 — LinkedIn-native format; appropriate length
- Authenticity: 8/15 — "incredible leaders," "so much" — vague; no specific person, lesson, or situation
- Impact: 9/15 — does not achieve any specific objective; generic leadership content
- Originality: 8/15 — every sentence could be from any LinkedIn post about leadership
- Reputation Safety: 10/10 — safe

**Total: 74/100 — Below threshold; requires revision**

**What to fix:** Replace every abstract claim with one specific experience or observation. Name the lesson. Name the leader (if appropriate). The question at the end is hollow — replace with a specific invitation.

## WHAT EDITORS LOOK FOR

A trained editorial eye identifies these quality signals in the first 30 seconds of reading:

**Positive signals:**
- ✓ Specific number, name, date, or outcome in the first two sentences
- ✓ First sentence creates one of: contradiction, curiosity, or recognition
- ✓ No sentence that could be removed without the piece losing something
- ✓ The register at the first sentence matches the register at the last
- ✓ The reader knows something they did not know before

**Negative signals:**
- ✗ Abstract opening ("In today's..." / "Success is..." / "I'm excited to...")
- ✗ Three or more banned phrases detected
- ✗ No specific information in first three sentences
- ✗ Tone drift between opening and closing
- ✗ Closing question that is not genuinely curious

## AUTOMATED CHECK LIST (Pre-Scoring)

Run these before scoring to identify quick disqualifiers:

1. Count banned phrases (from `engines/anti-ai-slop-engine.md`): If > 3 → score Authenticity at most 8/15
2. Check first line: Is it specific? If not → score Impact at most 10/15
3. Check length vs. platform spec: If wrong → score Platform Fit at most 10/15
4. Check first and last sentence: Same register? If not → score Tone Fit at most 11/15
5. Check for any reputation flags: If flagged → score Reputation Safety based on severity
